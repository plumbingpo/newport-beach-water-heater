Many of the installation calls we get in Newport Beach, CA are not replacements at all. A household outgrows its tank and needs more capacity. A homeowner wants the heater out of a hall closet and into the garage or basement. A remodel needs a second heater on the far side of the house, or a move to all-electric swaps a gas unit for a heat pump model. What these jobs share is new work: gas lines that must be sized for the load, vents that must be routed and pitched correctly, circuits that must match the heater, and water lines that must be run cleanly. Newport Beach Water Heater works all of that out before the installation starts.

Sizing and placement shape most of the outcome. For a tank, we compare first-hour ratings against your household's busiest hour; for a tankless unit, we size by flow rate and winter temperature rise. Then comes placement. A gas heater needs a vent route and combustion air, a heat pump heater needs open space and a condensate drain, and a whole-house electric tankless unit usually needs a big dedicated circuit and matching panel capacity. Put a heater above a finished ceiling and it needs a drain pan; put a gas unit in a garage and code may require a raised platform. We explain the trade-offs plainly and without pressure.

On installation day, every step gets verified. New gas piping is run and pressure-tested, a sediment trap goes on, and the vent is routed exactly to spec. For electric and heat pump units, the circuit is matched to the load. Water lines get accessible shutoffs and proper metal transitions, and the relief valve discharge runs full size to a safe point. Code items like expansion tanks, drain pans, and seismic straps are added where required, and we handle the permit when your jurisdiction needs one. Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

A few simple habits, like an annual flush and a quick look at the relief valve, can add years to a tank. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • A 120-degree setting protects against burns and slows mineral buildup
  • Start a flushing routine early, before sediment has a chance to harden
  • Tankless heaters need a yearly flush to clear scale from the heat exchanger
  • Schedule regular maintenance, including an anode rod check
  • Glance at the pan and discharge pipe every month or two; water there means it is time to call
  • Leave working space around the heater, and never stack storage against it
  • Nothing flammable belongs near a gas burner or its air supply
