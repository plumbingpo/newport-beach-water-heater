Getting More Years From Your Water Heater in Costa Mesa

Most early failures start with maintenance that never happened, and the habits that prevent them are simple. Flush the tank every year to clear sediment, have the anode rod inspected every few years, and operate the relief valve annually so it doesn't seize. Set the thermostat close to 120 degrees to reduce scald risk and slow scale buildup. If you have a tankless unit, descale it yearly where the water is hard to protect the heat exchanger.

How old is your water heater? The rating plate on the tank usually includes the month and year it was built, and that date says a lot about what comes next. Most tank heaters make it to 8 to 12 years, heat pump models 10 to 15, and regularly descaled tankless units about 20. If your heater is beyond that range and also shows rusty water, rumbling, or dampness under the tank, it's usually due for a planned replacement, and that's almost always cheaper and less stressful than a rushed emergency install after a leak.

Left alone, a small problem tends to grow. Take a spent anode rod: replacing it is a quick, affordable repair, but if nobody replaces it, the water starts eating the tank steel, and suddenly a minor fix has become a full replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to keep our number in their phone and call at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves money and spares you a surprise cleanup.
